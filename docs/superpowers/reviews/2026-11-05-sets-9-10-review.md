# Sets 9 & 10 ("Still Places"): final review findings

**Date:** 2026-11-05
**Task:** Sets 9 & 10 plan, Task 14 (the release step left out).
**Branch:** `s9-t14`, cut from `sets-13-14` at `7b3b04e` (CI green there).
**Read against:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-sets-9-10-design.md` (§ below) and the plan's Review focus.

Severity is graded by what Michael would hear or see:
- **Critical**: breaks the app, a set, a save or the photosensitivity rules.
- **Important**: a spec target or a Review focus item fails in normal playing.
- **Minor**: a small deviation, or a failure that needs an unusual setup.

## Findings

1. **`Sources/InStrumDSP/Synth.cpp:353`** (the CC 121 case in `handleMidi`). Reset All Controllers set channel pressure to zero but left poly pressure on. After a reset, a koto note held under its key's own pressure kept its yuri (13.9 cents measured), and a shakuhachi note kept its meri, up to 150 cents flat. This is Review focus 1's failure mode ("a flute flat"). Only a keyboard that sends poly pressure and CC 121 can trigger it. The app's own panic sends CC 123 and 120, which already reset everything. **Minor. Fixed:** CC 121 now also sends `aftertouch(note, 0)` for all 128 notes. Engines that ignore pressure do nothing with it. Tests: `testResetAllControllersStopsTheYuri` and `testResetAllControllersLetsPolyPressureGo`.
2. **`Sources/InStrumCore/Worlds/BedroomDawnModel.swift:195`** (the `indoors` of `ambience(roles:open:heldDawn:)`). Spec §5 says that in rime everything outdoors is muffled a little (indoors + 0.15). Every world with a sky adds `WeatherAmbience.indoorsLift(sky)` except Bedroom Window at Dawn, so its street, breeze and birds came through the sash at the same level whatever the rime. **Minor. Fixed:** indoors is now the window's own value plus the lift. Test: `testBedroomHushesInRime`.
3. **`Sources/InStrumCore/Worlds/ZenRockGardenModel.swift:325, 337`**. Spec §9.1 says the garden's temple bell is "rarer: density 0.6". The ambience has one density per scene, so the garden's bell strikes as often as the tea house's (every 120–300 s ÷ the mood's density). Lowering the scene's density would also slow the water-knocker and the warbler. **Minor. Deferred:** the fix needs a per-source density in `AmbienceScene`, `AmbienceAU` and the C API. That is a restructure for one source.
4. **`Sources/InStrumDSP/ZenShakuhachi.hpp:36–37`**. The spec and the brief give the range as D4–D6. The engine plays D4–D7, and notes above D7 fold down. **Minor. Left as is:** this was a deliberate choice, recorded in `da14d7d`. Nothing in the demo goes above C6.
5. **`Sources/InStrumApp/Visuals/Worlds/AuroraFjordShaders.swift:397–399`**. Spec §6.4 puts rime on "the fjord's rail and village roofs". The jetty posts carry rime; the far village roofs do not. **Minor. Deferred:** far silhouettes were skipped deliberately (`0bf45f8`, `55e654c`), and the roofs are a few pixels tall.
6. **`Sources/InStrumDSP/TeaHouseKoto.cpp:199`**. The wheel's lower half bends a koto string down by up to 200 cents. Spec §4.2 describes the press only raising the pitch. **Observation, not a defect:** a pitch wheel bends both ways by MIDI convention, and the spec's research mentions hiki-iro, which lowers a note. Noted for Michael's ear.
7. **`Sources/InStrumCore/Worlds/ZenRockGardenModel.swift:8–11`**. The water-knocker on screen and the one in the ambience keep separate cycles, so the clack you see is not the clack you hear. Spec §9.1 does not tie them, and the model documents the choice. **Observation:** on the ear-and-eye list, and a candidate for the next run.
8. **Temple bell tuning in every key (Review A check).** `testTempleBellSitsOnTheKey` measures only D2. On the g++ mirror, all 12 roots measure within 1.0 cent of the key on the mid channel. The strike was rendered through the full ambience (room included) at closeness 0.25, 0.55 and 0.75. In one ear alone at G♯2, the room's modulated tail beats against the hum at 0.2–0.4 Hz, so a 4 s window can read up to 12 cents off. The hum's pitch is unchanged; this sounds like a bell's own beating. **No action.**

## Checked and found sound

**Review A: DSP, ambience, audio**
- **The cores** (`PluckedString.hpp`, `BreathWind.hpp`):
  - The Lagrange reads stay inside the ring buffers at every pitch asked for (C1 a whole tone down, −50 cents, full yuri; MIDI 36 under full meri, the wheel and fine tune).
  - Each glide lasts exactly the rendered block.
  - Loop gains stay at or below unity at DC.
  - The sympathetic bank's block (64) is wider than the koto's inner block (32).
  - `BreathWind::render` is called with n ≤ 64.
- **No allocation on the audio thread.** Every voice, bank, body, echo and wall line is sized at construction, and per-block scratch is on the stack. The same holds for the temple bell, paper screens, water-knocker and warbler: no heap use in `render`, and the key, strikes and debug hooks go through atomics.
- **No NaN or Inf.** A sweep on the g++ mirror covered both engines: every third note, every dial at 0 and 1, every mood axis at 0 and 1, transpose ±12, fine tune ±50, the wheel full down and up, channel and poly pressure, CC 121 and 120, and new notes at 0 and 127. It ran at 22.05, 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz. All output was finite and at or below 0 dBFS. Every `pow`, `log` and `sqrt` in the new DSP takes a positive argument by construction.
- **Voices within limits.** The koto plays 24 voices (pool 30) and the shakuhachi 8 (pool 10). A stolen voice fades over 20 ms. Retriggering a string hands it over in 20 ms.
- **Pitch bend and pressure as specced** (spec §4.6):
  - The wheel decodes as 14 bits, centre 8192, ±2 semitones at the ends.
  - 0xD0 sends channel pressure to every note; 0xA0 sends poly pressure to one key.
  - Koto: the press glides through two 4 ms smoothers.
  - Koto yuri: 4–6 Hz, ±15 cents × pressure × Yuri, starting after 150 ms.
  - Shakuhachi meri: down to −150 cents × Bend, the reflection low-pass 5 → 1.8 kHz.
  - Shakuhachi kari: +100 cents on the wheel's first semitone up.
  - Engines older than this run ignore bend and pressure (pinned).
- **Review focus 1.** `allNotesOff` (the C API, CC 123 and panic) puts the wheel and both kinds of pressure back to rest on both engines, and new notes start neutral. Switching instrument builds a fresh engine. Switching preset or world keeps the same engine, so a wheel still held stays held, which is correct. The one gap was CC 121 (finding 1).
- **Review focus 2.** The bell's f0 is taken from the key at the moment of each strike and never changes while it rings (pinned). A key that cannot be sensed leaves the last root (E by default).
- **Spec numbers:**
  - Koto: dials, presets, voices, Space, Width and mood mapping match §4.2. Scrape from velocity 118. Thump above velocity 90. Body modes at 100/194/220/285 Hz.
  - Shakuhachi: Air 60–350 ms, release 150–400 ms, Breath −24…−6 dB, muraiki ≥ 112 (2–6 kHz, 120 ms), yuri 3.2–4.8 Hz at 8–20 cents from 0.4 s, Bamboo formants at 600 Hz and 1.9 kHz (Q 3) with a 3 ms click, echo −14 dB at 0.42 and 0.78 s.
  - The four ambience sources match §5.
- **Panic.** `allSoundOff` silences both engines within 50 ms, with the room, the echo and the wall cleared and the 20 ms fades kept out of them (pinned).

**Review B: sky, worlds, sets, app**
- **Smooth tier.**
  - `AutoQuality` caps Smooth at 1 MP.
  - Both world shaders use analytic silhouettes: ray–ellipsoid stones and segment distances, with no per-pixel marching. The garden's ridge shadow uses 2–3 fixed taps.
  - `fbm` uses at most 3 octaves, and 2 on Smooth (the tea house and garden noise, iridescence's `fbm3c` and the sky's).
  - Wisp and rake budgets drop on Smooth.
  - No buffers are made per frame: `setFragmentBytes`, and the rake field is a ring of three textures, re-uploaded only when its version moves, at most once a frame.
- **FlashLimiter.**
  - The tea steam's held glow goes through the exposure limiter.
  - The garden's chord wave has its own limiter.
  - Wisps, puffs, held rakes and glints are scaled by the limiter's sparkle (halved on gentle).
  - The candle and brazier flicker is small and continuous, as the cabin's fire is.
- **No `pow(` of a possibly negative base.** The tea house and garden shaders have no `pow` at all. The new sky code has none (`testShadersSquareWithoutPow` covers `fbm3c`, `iridescentCloud` and the rime helpers). `mslcheck` reports every shader OK.
- **Iridescence** (§6.3):
  - Only on light-cloud spells (one in two at random at 0.3–1; 0.6 when picked), on thin cover 0.15–0.6, with the sun 5–50° up.
  - The band is one `sin` and `cos` of a dot product. It peaks 10–20° from the sun and fades by 40°.
  - It is clamped to the cloud's own light + 20%.
  - The pileus is one extra octave read.
  - Reflections get it through `skyRadiance`.
- **Rime** (§6.2, §6.4):
  - The exact per-step solution builds with τ 12 min and melts with τ 6 min in sun or 40 min in the dark, both divided by Rime speed.
  - It stays in 0…1 over hours (Review focus 3, pinned) and resets with the sky.
  - The fringe is 0.6% of the view × rime on the windward (left-facing) edge, lit by the horizon × 1.3.
  - Ice-fog drift uses the crystal ring.
  - The wind drops in rime (except a blizzard's).
- **The two worlds:** parameters, ambience levels, closeness, moods, the cold rule at the tea house, seasons by date and hemisphere, the maple's colours, snow in the grooves, and the warbler only on spring and summer mornings, all as §8 and §9 specify (apart from finding 3).
- **Paper screen.** `WindowSpec.Material.paperScreen` has no blur and no reflection. Frost reads as ice on the panes, and the kumiko are pale wood.
- **Older saves** (Review focus 4):
  - `InStrumSet.creditID` is optional; old JSON with a credit, `null` or none decodes.
  - `SkySettings` gives its two new keys defaults on decode, clamped.
  - `WeatherKind.rime` is appended.
  - Settings store the active set by id.
- **Place cards** (Review focus 5). Both sets draw distinct place cards: 13 strings and 5 bamboo rings, a 60/48 s pulse, the name and mood instead of song credits, and no Listen button. The gallery tints match §11.
- **Original work only.** Both demo phrases are original: hirajōshi on E, and the five notes D, F, G, A, C. All sound is synthesis; there are no samples. Card art is drawn in code. No UI or release-note text uses "exotic" or similar shorthand. The instruments' techniques are named as playing techniques.
